# "Who's speaking?" Chatbots and how to understand them

**Reading Seminar · 15 Weeks · MSc Cognitive Systems, Uni Potsdam**

~David Schlangen, 2026-04-22~

---

## Course Description

This seminar examines AI chatbots as a social, epistemic, and philosophical phenomenon. Rather than focusing on technical architecture, we will take a step back and look at them as socio-technical systems, asking how we should think about them as such. The semester moves from empirical ground — how people actually use and respond to chatbots, and what harms have been documented — through the social-cognition mechanisms that make anthropomorphism nearly inevitable, into the competing metaphorical framings that researchers have proposed, and then into the philosophical machinery of speech acts and personhood. The course ends by turning back to practice: what can designers, researchers, and policymakers do to counter the most harmful dynamics?

The seminar is reading-intensive. Each week has one designated **anchor** reading that sets the agenda, supported by two to five **core** readings. All readings are required. Students are expected to come prepared to discuss, not merely summarise.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

- Critically evaluate competing metaphors and framings for AI language systems.
- Connect empirical evidence about chatbot use and harm to theoretical frameworks in communication studies, philosophy, and cognitive science.
- Apply speech-act theory and philosophy of mind to the question of what LLMs do when they "speak."
- Situate individual interaction effects within broader societal dynamics of homogenisation, institutional erosion, and epistemic monoculture.
- Propose and defend evidence-based counter-design principles aimed at mitigating anthropomorphism and epistemic harm.

## Conventions

**Anchor reading:** the paper designated as anchor sets the agenda for that session and should be read first. Students should prepare a brief (3–5 sentence) written summary of its central argument before class.

**Core readings:** all other listed readings are required. They are chosen to support, complicate, or extend the anchor.

### Part I — Landscape: What Are People Actually Doing With Chatbots?

---

#### Week 1 · How people use chatbots — the data

**Discussion:** What is surprising (or not) in the usage distribution? Does the gap between imagined and actual use affect how we should theorise about these systems?

#### Week 2 · Teens, companions, and emotional attachment

> *Companionship is a major use pattern, especially among younger users. What does attachment to a chatbot look like, and what does it involve?*

**Discussion:** Is emotional attachment to a chatbot a category error, an anthropological fact, or both? What responsibilities follow for designers and policymakers?

**Discussion:** Does cognitive offloading to LLMs differ qualitatively from earlier tool use (search engines, calculators)? What would "healthy" AI-assisted thinking look like?

#### Week 4 · Adverse and clinical effects: sycophancy, delusion, psychosis risk

**Discussion:** Is the chatbot causing harm, or revealing and amplifying pre-existing vulnerabilities? Does that distinction matter morally or legally?

### Part IV — Philosophy: Speech, Assertion, Personhood

---
